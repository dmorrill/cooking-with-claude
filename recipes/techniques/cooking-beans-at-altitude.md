# Cooking Beans at Altitude (5,000+ ft)

Technique guide for cooking dried beans at high altitude. Tested at 5,280 ft.

Beans take noticeably longer at altitude — water boils at ~202°F at 5,280 ft instead of 212°F, so there's less heat driving the softening. Learned the hard way on a batch of heirloom beans (Rio Zape, Moro, Christmas Lima).

## The rules that matter most
1. **Lids on.** The #1 lesson. Cooking uncovered runs the pot cooler *and* boils off water fast, so beans creep above the waterline and go crusty/leathery on top while staying hard. A lid holds heat, cuts evaporation, and keeps them submerged — dramatically faster. (Peek after covering: a lidded pot can jump to a hard boil — nudge the heat down.)
2. **Gentle simmer, never a hard boil.** Bare bubbles. A rolling boil tumbles beans and shreds skins (Christmas Limas split especially easily). Low and slow protects shape — important for salads.
3. **Stay submerged.** Keep 1–2 inches of liquid over the beans; top up with **hot** water (cold shocks them firm).
4. **Salt late.** Add salt in roughly the last 15–20 min, to taste (broth should taste like a light seasoned soup).
5. **Expect a long window + test often.** Even after a soak, a big bean can run well past a sea-level estimate (plan on ~15–30% longer). Start tasting early and pull each pot the moment it's *creamy through, skins tender* — not past. Off the heat they stop cooking and firm up slightly as they cool, so pulling right at tender is perfect.

## Soak note
A **full 8-hr soak** hydrates better than a **quick-soak** (2-min boil + 1-hr rest), and better-hydrated beans cook faster. At altitude the quick-soak stretches the simmer time further — plan for it, or do the long soak when you can.

## Stubborn-pot rescue (use sparingly)
A **small pinch of baking soda (⅛–¼ tsp per pot)** raises the pH and counteracts both the altitude and hard water — good for a pot that's stalled hard. **But it weakens skins and can push toward mush fast**, so:
- Skip it for salad beans if patience will do (protects shape).
- If used, test every 8–10 min after adding, and don't overdo it (too much = mushy, faintly soapy).

## Save the broth
The starchy cooking liquid is worth keeping (silky, great for pilaf/soup). Dump only the initial **soak** water; keep the **cooking** liquid. Delicate pale broths (e.g., Christmas Lima) are worth saving separately from dark hearty ones (Moro, Rio Zape).
