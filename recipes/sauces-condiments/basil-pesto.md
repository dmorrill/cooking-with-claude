# Basil Pesto (Classic Genovese-style)
- **Prep Time**: 10 minutes
- **Cook/Process Time**: 5 minutes (toasting pine nuts + blitzing)
- **Total Time**: ~15 minutes
- **Yield**: ~¾ cup (from 62 g basil)
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Type**: Condiment / Sauce (Italian) / Flavor Multiplier
- **Source**: Elle's kitchen — scalable by-weight ratio

### About
A bright, fresh basil pesto built from a **by-weight ratio** so it scales to whatever basil you've got. Toss with pasta, spoon over roasted vegetables or eggs, swirl into soups, or spread on sandwiches.

**The ratio (per 100 g basil leaves):**
- Pine nuts = leaves × 0.5
- Grated Parmesan = leaves × 0.7
- Olive oil = leaves × 1.2

This batch is scaled for **62 g basil leaves** (amounts below).

### Ingredients
- [ ] **Fresh basil leaves** (62 g) - *Location: garden / refrigerator*
- [ ] **Pine nuts** (31 g) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Grated Parmesan** (43 g) - *Location: refrigerator*
- [ ] **Extra-virgin olive oil** (74 g / ≈ 80 ml) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Garlic cloves** (1–2) - *Location: counter / pantry*
- [ ] **Salt** (~½ tsp, to taste) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Lemon juice** (optional, a squeeze) - *Location: fridge / counter lemons*

### Equipment
- [ ] **Small dry frying pan** (to toast pine nuts)
- [ ] **Food processor** (or mortar and pestle)
- [ ] **Small pot + bowl of ice water** (optional, for blanching basil)

### Instructions

#### 1. Toast the Pine Nuts
1. Dry pan over medium-low heat. Add pine nuts and toss 3–4 minutes until golden and fragrant.
2. Watch closely — they burn fast. Cool before using so they don't turn the pesto oily.

#### 2. Blanch the Basil (optional, but worth it)
1. Drop basil leaves into boiling water for 5–10 seconds, then straight into ice water.
2. Squeeze dry. This locks in the bright green and keeps it from oxidizing to army-drab.
3. Skip if you want it faster and don't mind the color dulling.

#### 3. Build the Paste
1. Food processor: garlic + cooled pine nuts + a pinch of salt. Pulse to a coarse crumb.
2. Add basil and pulse again.

#### 4. Stream in the Oil
1. With the motor running, drizzle the olive oil in slowly until it comes together.
2. Don't over-process — a little texture beats a smooth purée, and over-blending heats it and dulls the flavor.

#### 5. Finish
1. Fold in the Parmesan **by hand** at the end.
2. Taste, adjust salt, add a squeeze of lemon if you want it brighter.

### Storage
- Store airtight with the surface flush and a thin film of oil on top. **Fridge ~5 days.**
- **Freezes well** in portions (ice-cube trays work). For best texture, leave the cheese out and stir it in after thawing.

### Uses
- Toss with hot pasta (loosen with a splash of pasta water)
- Spoon over roasted vegetables, eggs, or grilled proteins
- Swirl into minestrone or tomato soup
- Spread on sandwiches or fold into grain bowls
