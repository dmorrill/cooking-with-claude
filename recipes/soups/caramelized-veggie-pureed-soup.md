# Caramelized Garden Veggie Soup with Miso

**A rich, deeply flavored pureed soup that turns a fridge full of vegetables into something special**

- **Prep Time**: 20 minutes
- **Cook Time**: 45-50 minutes
- **Total Time**: ~70 minutes
- **Servings**: 6-8
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Type**: Soup, Batch Cooking, Fridge Cleanout
- **Source**: Original recipe

## Ingredients

### Vegetables
- All your tomatoes (roma, cherry, heirloom — roughly chopped, ~2-3 lbs)
- Red onions (2+, roughly chopped)
- Bell peppers (red and green, 3-4, roughly chopped)
- Leek (1 large, cleaned and sliced — white and light green parts)
- Celery (3-4 stalks, roughly chopped)
- 4-5 cloves garlic (or use pre-cut garlic from fridge)

### For Caramelizing
- 3-4 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- Black pepper to taste
- 1 teaspoon sugar (optional — helps caramelization)

### For the Soup
- 4-6 cups homemade veggie broth from your batch
- 1-2 tablespoons white miso (stir in at the end — don't boil after adding)
- Fresh parsley (big handful, stems and all — goes in before pureeing)

### For Serving
- Labneh or sour cream (dollop on top)
- Fresh dill (chopped, as garnish)
- Good olive oil for drizzling
- Crusty bread

## Instructions

### 1. Prep the Vegetables (15 min)
1. Roughly chop everything — doesn't need to be pretty since it's all getting pureed
2. Keep the tomatoes separate from the other veg (they release a lot of liquid)
3. Clean the leek well (dirt hides between layers)

### 2. Caramelize (25-30 min)
1. Heat olive oil in your largest heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over **medium-high heat**
2. Add onions, peppers, leek, and celery with salt and pepper
3. Cook, stirring every few minutes, for **15-20 minutes** until deeply golden and starting to stick to the pot
4. If things are browning too fast, reduce to medium
5. Add garlic, cook 1 minute
6. Add tomatoes and optional sugar
7. Cook another **8-10 minutes** — tomatoes will break down and deglaze the caramelized bits on the bottom
8. Stir and scrape up all the good brown stuff (fond)

### 3. Simmer (15 min)
1. Add veggie broth — start with 4 cups, you can add more later for desired consistency
2. Toss in the fresh parsley (stems included — they have tons of flavor)
3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer
4. Cook **15 minutes** until everything is very soft

### 4. Puree
1. Use an immersion blender directly in the pot (easiest)
2. Or transfer in batches to a blender — **leave the lid slightly cracked** to vent steam
3. Blend until completely smooth
4. Add more broth if too thick

### 5. Finish with Miso
1. Ladle out ~1/4 cup of warm (not boiling) soup into a small bowl
2. Whisk in 1-2 tablespoons white miso until dissolved
3. Stir the miso mixture back into the pot
4. **Do not boil after adding miso** — kills the good stuff
5. Taste and adjust salt/pepper

### 6. Serve
1. Ladle into bowls
2. Top with a dollop of labneh or sour cream
3. Scatter fresh dill on top
4. Drizzle with good olive oil
5. Serve with crusty bread

## Notes & Tips

### Key Techniques
- **Don't rush the caramelization** — this is where all the flavor comes from. Let things get really golden-brown.
- **Scrape the fond** — those browned bits on the bottom of the pot are pure flavor. The tomato liquid will deglaze them.
- **Miso goes in last** — boiling destroys the enzymes and dulls the flavor. Stir it into warm (not hot) soup.

### Storage
- Refrigerator: 5 days in airtight container
- Freezer: 3 months (freeze without the miso — stir it in when reheating)
- Great for meal prep — portion into jars for easy lunches

### Variations
- **Smoky version**: Add 1 tsp smoked paprika with the garlic
- **Spicy**: Add a chopped jalapeño or red pepper flakes during caramelization
- **Creamy**: Stir in a splash of coconut cream or cashew cream before serving
- **Heartier**: Serve over rice or with grilled cheese
