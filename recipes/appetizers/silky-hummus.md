# Silky Restaurant-Quality Hummus
- **Prep Time**: 15 minutes (plus 15-20 min to soften canned chickpeas, or overnight soak + 1 hr cook for dried)
- **Process Time**: 5-8 minutes
- **Total Time**: ~30 minutes (canned)
- **Yield**: ~2.5 cups (6-8 servings as a dip)
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Type**: Appetizer / Dip / Plant-Based
- **Source**: Developed June 2026 — technique based on the Israeli-style method (overcook chickpeas + whip tahini base before adding chickpeas)

### Why This Works
Two techniques make the difference between gritty and silky:
1. **Overcook the chickpeas with baking soda** so the skins float free and the beans fall apart.
2. **Whip the tahini with lemon and ice water FIRST** — before the chickpeas ever go in — to build a creamy emulsified base.

### Ingredients
- [ ] **Garbanzo beans / chickpeas** (1 can or ~1.5 cups cooked) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Tahini** (½ cup) - *Location: pantry/fridge*
- [ ] **Lemon** (1, juiced, ~3 Tbsp) - *Location: fridge*
- [ ] **Garlic** (1-2 cloves, grated or minced) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Ground cumin** (½ tsp) - *Location: spice rack*
- [ ] **Salt** (¾ tsp, to taste) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Baking soda** (¼ tsp, for softening chickpeas) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Ice water** (2-4 Tbsp) - *Location: tap + ice*

#### To Finish
- [ ] **Good olive oil** (for drizzling) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Paprika or sumac** (for dusting) - *Location: spice rack*
- [ ] **Fresh parsley** (or dill, chopped) - *Location: fridge/herbs*

### Equipment
- [ ] **Food processor** (the workhorse choice — handles thick low-liquid mixtures better than a blender; no tamping)
- [ ] **Small pot** (to soften chickpeas)
- [ ] **Bowl of cold water** (for skinning)
- [ ] **Microplane or garlic press**
- [ ] **Serving bowl** (light-colored)

### Instructions

#### 1. Soften the Chickpeas
1. Drain the chickpeas. Put them in a pot with water + ¼ tsp baking soda.
2. Simmer 15-20 minutes until they're mushy and the skins start floating free. (For dried: soak overnight, then cook ~1 hr until very soft.)
3. The baking soda breaks down the skins — this is the single biggest texture upgrade.

#### 2. Remove the Skins (~80% is plenty)
1. Drain into a bowl of cold water.
2. Swish vigorously with your hands — skins detach and float to the surface.
3. Skim floating skins off, pour off the cloudy water, repeat once or twice.
4. Don't chase every last skin — ~80% removed gets you 95% of the way to smooth, and the long process step pulverizes the stragglers.

#### 3. Tame the Garlic
1. Grate/mince 1-2 cloves into the lemon juice.
2. Let it sit ~10 minutes so the raw garlic mellows and won't taste harsh.

#### 4. Whip the Tahini Base (the key step)
1. In the food processor: tahini + lemon-garlic juice + salt + cumin.
2. Run it — **it will seize up thick and pasty. This is supposed to happen.**
3. With the processor running, drizzle in ice water 1 Tbsp at a time until it turns pale, smooth, and creamy (~2-4 Tbsp). Cold water helps it emulsify lighter and fluffier.

#### 5. Add the Chickpeas
1. Add the warm, softened, de-skinned chickpeas.
2. Process a full **3-4 minutes** — set a timer, it's longer than feels right.
3. Scrape down the sides once or twice; add a splash more ice water if too thick.

#### 6. Taste & Plate
1. Adjust: more salt? more lemon (brightness)? more cumin? Keep it slightly looser than you think — it firms up in the fridge.
2. Spoon into a bowl, use the back of the spoon to make a swooshed well.
3. Pool olive oil in the well, dust with paprika or sumac, scatter parsley/herbs.

### Variations
- **Za'atar** dusted on top
- **Harissa** swirled in for heat
- **Green hummus** — fold in chopped dill/parsley at the end
- **Roasted garlic** instead of raw for a sweeter, mellower flavor

### Storage
- Refrigerate in an airtight container up to **4-5 days**.
- Flavor and texture are often best the next day.
- Freezes well up to 3 months (stir in a little olive oil after thawing).

### Notes
- **Blender vs. food processor:** A high-speed blender gets marginally smoother but fights the thick mixture (climbs sides, traps air, needs tamping + more liquid). Food processor is the reliable default and texture difference is minimal once you've softened/skinned the chickpeas and processed long enough.
