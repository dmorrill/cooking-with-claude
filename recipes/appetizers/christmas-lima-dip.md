# Smoky Christmas Lima Dip

**Source:** Original recipe · **Makes:** ~2 cups (snacking for 2) · **Difficulty:** Easy · **Prep:** ~10 min + chill
Smooth, cold white-bean dip showing off the buttery Christmas Lima. Whole-food plant-based base; olive oil drizzled at the table.

## Ingredients
- 1½–2 cups cooked **Christmas Lima beans** (Rancho Gordo or similar heirloom)
- 1 **garlic clove** *(raw = punchy; microwave 15 sec or use a roasted clove for mellow)*
- 2 Tbsp fresh **lemon juice**
- 1–2 tsp **white miso** *(savory + salt backbone — start with 1)*
- ½–1 tsp **smoked paprika**, plus more to garnish
- ¼ tsp ground **cumin** (optional)
- 2–4 Tbsp **bean cooking broth** (from the Christmas Limas), to loosen
- Salt, to taste

**To serve**
- Good **extra-virgin olive oil** to drizzle *(a peppery medium-fruity oil, or a lemon-infused one for citrus pop)*
- Chopped **parsley or cilantro**
- Crudité: radishes, cucumbers, rainbow carrots, bell pepper strips, celery

## Method
1. Blend beans, garlic, lemon juice, miso, smoked paprika, and cumin in a food processor/blender until completely smooth, scraping down.
2. With the motor running, add **bean broth a splash at a time** until silky and scoopable — not stiff, not runny.
3. **Taste and adjust:** more miso/salt for savoriness, lemon for brightness, smoked paprika for smoke.
4. **Chill ≥30 min** (better made ahead — it firms slightly and the flavors meld).
5. Serve cold: spread in a shallow bowl, swoosh the top, **drizzle olive oil**, dust with smoked paprika, scatter herbs. Surround with crudité.

## Notes
- **Great use for a pot of home-cooked heirloom beans** — and their delicate broth. See [Cooking Beans at Altitude](../techniques/cooking-beans-at-altitude.md).
- **Oil at the table, not in the blender** keeps the olive oil's pepper/citrus notes fresh and the base itself oil-free.
- **Make-ahead friendly:** prep in the morning, snack in the afternoon. Keeps ~3–4 days refrigerated.
- **Variations:** sub **Ayocote Blanco** (also creamy/white); for a darker, earthier dip use **Rio Zape** or **Moro** with lime instead of lemon and extra cumin.
