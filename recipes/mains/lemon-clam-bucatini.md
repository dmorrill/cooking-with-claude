# Creamy Lemon Bucatini with Littleneck Clams
- **Prep Time**: 15 minutes active (+ 60-90 minutes clam purging, mostly hands-off)
- **Cook Time**: 20-25 minutes
- **Total Time**: ~2 hours with purging, ~40 minutes if clams are already purged
- **Yield**: 2 servings
- **Difficulty**: Medium (timing + salt management)
- **Type**: Dinner/Main Course
- **Source**: Elle's recipe, first cooked August 2026

A light but genuinely creamy clam pasta with garlic, shallot, lemon, parsley, and concentrated
clam liquor. The sauce should be silky and briny rather than thick or Alfredo-like.

**Tested:** 16 littlenecks, 14 opened (at 5,280 ft).

### Ingredients

#### Clams
- [ ] **Littleneck clams** (16) - *Location: buy day-of; store in fridge in an open bowl, never sealed or in water*
- [ ] **Kosher salt** (for purging, ~2 Tbsp per quart water) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Dry white wine** (1/4 cup) or water (3 Tbsp) - *Location: refrigerator*

#### Pasta
- [ ] **Bucatini** (6 oz / 170 g) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Lightly salted water** - *see altitude note below*

#### Sauce
- [ ] **Olive oil or butter** (1 tsp) - *Location: pantry / refrigerator*
- [ ] **Shallot** (1½ Tbsp, very finely minced) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Garlic** (1 small clove, finely minced or grated) - *Location: counter*
- [ ] **Heavy cream** (1/3 cup) - *Location: refrigerator*
- [ ] **Reserved strained clam liquor** (to taste) - *from the steaming step*
- [ ] **Lemon zest** (1/2–1 tsp, finely grated) - *Location: counter*
- [ ] **Fresh parsley** (1 Tbsp finely chopped, plus more to finish) - *Location: refrigerator*
- [ ] **Black pepper** (freshly ground, plenty) - *Location: pantry*
- [ ] **Parmesan** (optional, very light finish) - *Location: refrigerator*

### Equipment
- [ ] **Large bowl** - *for saltwater purging*
- [ ] **Wide skillet with lid** - *for steaming clams, then for the sauce*
- [ ] **Very fine strainer or cheesecloth** - *for straining grit from the clam liquor*
- [ ] **Tongs** - *pulling clams the moment they open*
- [ ] **Pasta pot + measuring cup** - *for reserving pasta water*

### Instructions

#### Step 1: Purge the Clams (60-90 minutes, hands-off)
1. Make a cold saltwater bath: ~2 Tbsp kosher salt per quart of water
2. Soak the clams 30-45 minutes
3. **Lift the clams out individually** — don't pour them off, or the expelled sand goes right back on them
4. Repeat with fresh saltwater for another 20-30 minutes; for especially sandy clams do a third round of 15-20 minutes
5. After the final purge, lift out, rinse, and scrub the shells under cold running water
6. Discard any cracked clams, or any that stay open when tapped

#### Step 2: Steam the Clams (5-8 minutes)
7. Arrange the clams in a single layer in a wide skillet
8. Add the wine (or water), cover, and set over medium-high to high heat
9. Start checking at about 2 minutes. **Pull each clam with tongs the second it opens** — they overcook fast
10. Give stubborn ones another minute or two; discard any that stay tightly shut
11. Let the liquid in the skillet settle a couple of minutes, then strain carefully through a very fine strainer, leaving the sediment behind
12. As the clams cool they'll release more liquor — save and strain that too
13. Taste the liquor. It may be *extremely* salty — treat it as concentrated seasoning, not stock
14. Pull the meat from the shells and refrigerate until the pasta is ready. **Do not rinse it**

#### Step 3: Cook the Bucatini (see altitude note)
15. Bring a pot of water to a boil and salt it **only lightly** — the clam liquor brings most of the seasoning
16. Cook the bucatini to about 2 minutes shy of al dente
17. **Reserve at least 1 cup of starchy pasta water** before draining

#### Step 4: Make the Sauce (6-8 minutes, while pasta cooks)
18. Heat the oil or butter in a wide skillet over medium-low
19. Add the shallot, cook 2-3 minutes until completely soft and translucent — no browning
20. Add the garlic, cook about 30 seconds
21. Add 1 Tbsp of the clam liquor and let it bubble briefly
22. Add the cream and bring to a gentle simmer; cook 2-3 minutes. **Don't reduce it to a thick cream sauce** — it should look slightly too loose for the finished pasta
23. Taste. For more clam flavor, add liquor 1 tsp at a time, tasting after every addition

#### Step 5: Finish the Pasta (3-4 minutes)
24. Move the bucatini into the skillet about 2 minutes before al dente
25. Add ~1/4 cup pasta water and toss vigorously over medium heat while the pasta finishes
26. Keep tossing until the cream, clam liquor, and starchy water emulsify into a glossy sauce that clings; loosen with a splash more pasta water if it tightens up
27. **Turn off the heat**
28. Add the clam meat, 1/2 tsp lemon zest, parsley, and plenty of black pepper
29. Toss in the residual heat ~30 seconds — just enough to warm the clams through
30. Taste and adjust: more zest, parsley, pepper, or a tiny bit of liquor
31. Serve immediately, with an extremely light grating of Parmesan if you want it

### Altitude Notes (High Altitude, 5,000+ ft)
- **Pasta**: water boils at ~202°F at 5,280 ft, so bucatini runs **~15-25% longer** than the box says.
  Go by bite, not the timer — "2 minutes shy of al dente" means 2 minutes shy of *your* tasted
  al dente, then finish in the pan
- **Pasta water**: it evaporates faster at altitude — start with a generous pot and keep the
  kettle warm in case you need to top up
- **Clams**: steam builds a touch slower, so expect the first shells to open closer to 3 minutes
  than 2. Watch the pan, not the clock — pull each one the moment it opens
- Everything else (sautéing the shallot, simmering the cream) is unchanged

### Pro Tips
- **Don't oversalt the pasta water.** The clam liquor can be surprisingly concentrated
- **Don't discard any clam liquid.** Both the steaming pan and the bowl of cooling clams hold
  good liquor — just strain carefully for grit
- **Add the clams last.** They're already cooked and turn rubbery quickly
- **The cream should be noticeable.** This isn't clam pasta with a splash of dairy — 1/3 cup
  cream gives it real richness, and pasta water keeps it silky rather than heavy
- **Lemon zest, not juice** — brightness without thinning the sauce or fighting the cream
- **Parmesan is optional.** Keep it very light so the littlenecks stay the dominant flavor

### Make This in the PNW
This is a **trip recipe** — it's best where the clams are freshest, so plan it into Pacific
Northwest travel rather than saving it for a landlocked kitchen.

- **Sourcing**: Puget Sound / Hood Canal littlenecks or Manila clams, bought same-day from a
  fish market or dock. Manilas are sweeter and thinner-shelled; they open faster, so start
  checking the pan at ~90 seconds instead of 2-3 minutes
- **Yield check**: High-altitude test run was 16 littlenecks → 14 opened (2 discarded). With truly
  fresh clams expect closer to 16/16, so 14-16 clams is enough for 2
- **Purging**: dock-fresh clams from a good vendor are often already purged — ask. If they are,
  one 20-30 minute soak is plenty and the whole recipe drops to ~40 minutes
- **At sea level**: pasta cooks on the box timing, water boils at 212°F, clams steam faster.
  Ignore the altitude notes above and pull everything a couple minutes earlier
- **Buy extra clams** if they're good — steamed clams with the liquor and bread is a fine
  second meal, and any leftover strained liquor freezes for a future batch of this sauce

### Storage & Reheating
- **Best eaten immediately** — cream sauces break and clams toughen on reheat
- Leftovers keep 1 day refrigerated; rewarm gently over low heat with a splash of cream or water
- Don't freeze
