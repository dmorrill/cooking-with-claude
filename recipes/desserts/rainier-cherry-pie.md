# Rainier Cherry Pie (High-Altitude)

**Sweet-cherry pie with a macerated, reduced-sugar filling and an all-butter high-altitude crust**

- **Prep Time**: 30 minutes active + overnight maceration
- **Cook Time**: 55-60 minutes
- **Cooling Time**: 3-4 hours (ideally overnight)
- **Total Time**: ~1 hour active + overnight macerate + overnight set
- **Yield**: 8 servings
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Type**: Dessert
- **Altitude**: Written for high altitude (5,000+ ft)
- **Status**: ⚠️ Developed but NOT YET TESTED — first attempt was scrapped when the macerated cherries sat too long and spoiled. Make within ~4 days of pitting.

## Description

Rainier cherries are a **sweet** cherry, not a tart pie cherry, so a standard cherry-pie-filling recipe (built for sour Montmorency) comes out cloying and flat. The fix: cut the sugar to roughly half, and lean on lemon juice for the acidity that makes it taste like *pie filling* rather than just sweet fruit.

The filling is **macerated overnight** (not cooked whole), which draws the juice out so you can thicken just the juice and fold the intact cherry halves back in — fresher flavor, halves that hold their shape. Maple sugar stands in for white sugar; it macerates just like granulated because it's dry/granular (maple *syrup* would add liquid and macerate poorly).

## Ingredients

### For the Filling (per ~1.5 lb / 681 g pitted, halved cherries)
- [ ] **Rainier cherries** (681 g / ~1.5 lb, pitted and halved)
- [ ] **Maple sugar** (3-4½ Tbsp, granular — or substitute granulated/coconut sugar)
- [ ] **Lemon juice** (1 Tbsp / juice of ½ lemon)
- [ ] **Salt** (pinch)
- [ ] **Cornstarch** (2¼ Tbsp — for thickening the drained juice)
- [ ] **Vanilla** (¼-½ tsp, optional, added off-heat)
- [ ] **Almond extract** (⅛-¼ tsp, optional — makes cherry taste *more* cherry)

**Per-pound scaling** (if the pitted weight differs): sugar 3 Tbsp/lb · cornstarch 1.5 Tbsp/lb · lemon 2 tsp/lb · pinch salt.

**Freezing the filling?** Swap cornstarch for the same amount of **tapioca starch or Clear Jel** — cornstarch weeps and breaks when frozen and thawed. Never cook-then-freeze a cornstarch filling.

### For the Crust (all-butter double crust, high-altitude)
- [ ] **All-purpose flour** (2½ cups / 300 g)
- [ ] **Sugar** (1 Tbsp)
- [ ] **Salt** (1 tsp)
- [ ] **Unsalted butter** (1 cup / 226 g / 2 sticks, cold, cubed ½")
- [ ] **Lemon juice or white vinegar** (1 tsp — tenderizer)
- [ ] **Ice water** (8-10 Tbsp — dry high-altitude flour usually needs the full 10)

### For Assembly
- [ ] **Egg** (1, lightly beaten for egg wash)
- [ ] **Butter** (1 Tbsp, to dot the filling)
- [ ] **Coarse sugar** (for sprinkling, optional)

## Equipment

- [ ] Mixing bowls
- [ ] Pastry cutter
- [ ] 9-inch pie plate
- [ ] Rolling pin
- [ ] Parchment + pie weights (or dried beans)
- [ ] Large baking sheet (foil-lined — filling *will* bubble over)
- [ ] Wire cooling rack

## Instructions

### Night before — Macerate
1. Toss cherries with maple sugar, lemon juice, salt (and almond extract if using). Cover, refrigerate overnight. **Hold the cornstarch and vanilla until baking day.**
2. Make the crust dough the same night (below) and chill.

### Make the crust
1. **Pre-chill everything** — cube butter and freeze 10 min; chill the flour bowl too (critical in a hot kitchen).
2. Whisk flour, sugar, salt. Cut in cold butter until pea- to walnut-sized bits remain — **leave visible butter chunks** for flakiness.
3. Add lemon/vinegar to the ice water. Drizzle in a few Tbsp at a time, tossing with a fork, until dough just holds when squeezed. At altitude, flour looks dry longer than expected — go to the full 10 Tbsp if needed.
4. Divide in two (one slightly larger = bottom). Flatten to disks, wrap, **chill ≥1 hour** (up to 2 days).

### Parbake the bottom shell
1. Roll the larger disk, fit into the pie plate, trim. **Freeze the shell 20-30 min** — a frozen shell barely shrinks.
2. Line with parchment, fill with pie weights. Bake at **400°F for 18 min**.
3. Remove weights + parchment, **brush bottom and sides with egg wash** (seals against sogginess), bake **5-7 min more** to pale gold. Cool.

### Thicken the filling (baking day, keep separate from the shell)
1. **Drain** cherries over a bowl (~¾-1 cup juice).
2. Whisk **2¼ Tbsp cornstarch into the *cold* drained juice** until smooth (cold first, or it clumps).
3. Cook over medium, stirring, until it bubbles and turns glossy/thick — 3-5 min.
4. Off heat, stir in vanilla. **Fold cherries back in — don't re-boil** (keeps halves intact). Cool.

### Assemble & final bake
1. **Assemble as late as possible** — ideally right before the final bake. A parbaked shell sitting filled for hours softens at the bottom even with the egg-wash seal.
2. Cooled filling into the shell, dot with 1 Tbsp butter. Add top crust or lattice; crimp, cut steam vents.
3. Egg wash the top, sprinkle coarse sugar.
4. Bake on the foil-lined sheet: **425°F for 20 min**, then **375°F for 30-35 min** until the filling bubbles *in the center* and the crust is deep golden. Shield edges with foil if browning fast.
5. **Cool 3-4 hours minimum, ideally overnight** — this is when the cornstarch sets. Cut it warm and you get cherry soup. Baking the night before serving is ideal timing.

## Notes & Tips

- **The lemon is non-negotiable** for Rainiers — low-acid, low-pigment cherries; the acid does double duty (flavor + set). Taste the raw mix; add a splash more if it's flat.
- **Maple sugar, not syrup** — granular macerates properly; syrup adds liquid.
- **Maple/yacon vs. white sugar** is a marginal blood-sugar improvement; the bigger levers are portion size and pairing slices with fat/protein. The cornstarch and cherries' own sugars dominate the glycemic load regardless of sweetener.
- **Yacon syrup**, if used, goes in *off the heat* (its prebiotic FOS degrades above ~250°F) and only in small amounts (>15-20 g causes GI distress); best as a 1-2 tsp finishing drizzle, not the main sweetener.
- **Hot-kitchen crust**: chill aggressively, work fast, don't let the butter melt into the flour.

## Storage

- **Room temperature**: 2 days loosely covered
- **Refrigerator**: up to 4-5 days
- **Reheat**: 10 min at 350°F to refresh the crust
- **Macerated raw cherries**: hold ~4-5 days refrigerated; freeze the raw macerated cherries (with headspace) if baking is delayed, then thaw and thicken fresh. Don't let them sit longer — the first test batch spoiled from sitting too long.
