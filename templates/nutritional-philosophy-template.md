# Nutritional Philosophy (template)

Copy this to `nutritional-philosophy.md` at the repo root and fill it in. It's the *why* behind your cooking system. Recipes and meal plans answer "what should I make?" This file answers "what am I trying to do with food?" Claude reads it before planning meals or suggesting recipes, so its suggestions start from your goals instead of from "what sounds good."

Keep it short and honest. A page you actually believe beats five pages of aspirations.

## Core Belief

*One or two sentences. What do you believe about food and health that makes this worth the effort?*

> Example: "Most of what I want from eating well is already known. What's been missing is the day-to-day layer between knowing it and doing it. This system is that layer."

---

## The Framework

Organize your beliefs in layers, from "what to eat" down to "how it shows up in my life." Name your sources so Claude can reason from them. Delete layers that don't apply.

### Layer 1: What to eat and why

*Source(s):* e.g. Dr. Michael Greger, *How Not to Die* (research free at [nutritionfacts.org](https://nutritionfacts.org))

*Key principle:* e.g. "Whole-food, plant-based as the foundation. Animal products aren't forbidden, but plants dominate."

*Operational checklist:* a concrete daily target Claude can count against. Greger's Daily Dozen is a good one:

| Category | Daily Servings | Serving Size |
|---|---|---|
| Beans | 3 | 1/2 cup cooked, 1/4 cup hummus |
| Berries | 1 | 1/2 cup fresh/frozen |
| Other Fruits | 3 | 1 medium fruit |
| Greens | 2 | 1 cup raw, 1/2 cup cooked |
| Cruciferous Vegetables | 1 | 1/2 cup chopped |
| Other Vegetables | 2 | 1/2 cup non-leafy |
| Flaxseed | 1 | 1 tbsp ground |
| Nuts & Seeds | 1 | 1/4 cup nuts |
| Herbs & Spices | 1 | 1/4 tsp turmeric |
| Whole Grains | 3 | 1/2 cup cooked |
| Beverages | 60 oz | Water, green or hibiscus tea |

### Layer 2: The binding constraint

*What has to be fixed first, before food quality matters much?* For some people it's blood sugar and insulin resistance (Dr. Jason Fung, *The Obesity Code*); for others it's sleep, time, or budget.

*Sequencing:* what you're working on now vs. later. "Fix the biggest constraint first, accept imperfection, refine later" is a useful rule to write down so Claude doesn't push you to change everything at once.

### Layer 3: How food affects how you feel today

*What feedback loops do you notice?* Examples:
- Blood sugar swings → mood swings
- Eating late → worse sleep → worse next day
- Big grain-heavy lunch → afternoon slump

These make nutrition feel immediate instead of a 30-year bet, and they give Claude concrete reasons to suggest one meal over another.

### Layer 4: Where you are now

*Optional, and only what you're comfortable writing down.* Your current phase (building habits, maintaining, a specific goal) and any tools you use (food log, CGM, sleep tracker). Claude plans differently for "maintaining" than for "actively working toward a goal."

## How This Shapes the Cooking System

### Meal planning priorities (in order)

1. *e.g. Support my current goal*
2. *e.g. Maximize Daily Dozen coverage*
3. *e.g. Keep blood sugar steady*
4. *e.g. Dinner early enough to close the eating window well before bed*
5. *e.g. Whole food, plant-forward*

### Per-meal screen

A fast rule for judging any one meal, whether home-cooked, delivered, or eaten out. Example:

- **Fiber per calorie:** does this fill me up without a lot of calories?
- **Color and plant variety:** more colors on the plate means more antioxidants. A beige plate is the miss.
- **Protein to a floor, not a maximum:** hit a daily minimum across meals; don't chase it at the cost of fiber.
- **A potassium anchor:** beans, greens, potatoes, squash, or fruit in every meal, with added sodium kept modest.

### Recipe evaluation questions

When suggesting or creating a recipe, Claude checks:
- How many Daily Dozen categories does it cover?
- Does it help or hurt blood sugar stability?
- Is it whole and minimally processed?
- Does it fit the current goal?

### Active trade-offs

*Things you enjoy that work against the framework, and how you want to handle them.* Example: "Alcohol undermines sleep and blood sugar, but the evening ritual matters. Default to suggesting non-alcoholic or low-ABV drinks that are worth making."

## For Claude

- Read this before meal planning, recipe suggestions, or weekly plans.
- Flag when a plan is light on plants or heavy on blood-sugar-spiking foods, as a note, not a lecture.
- Be pragmatic, not purist. The framework serves the person.
- Note meal-timing trade-offs when scheduling dinners on the calendar.
